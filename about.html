Ten minutes, three acts

Do them in order. Each takes a few minutes, and the third only works because you did the first two — which is rather the point.

Act one · about two minutes

Arrive as a stranger

Nothing is known about you. No account, no history, no useful cookie. This is the hardest state for personalization, and it is where most of the industry gives up and shows everyone the same thing.

Do this. Search using a word rather than a product name — a colour, a material, a category. Then open a collection from the nav and use the filters.

  • One card per product, not one per variant. The same item in eight sizes is a single result, with the sizes as chips underneath.
  • The colour swatches are real. They come from the product feed, not from someone hand-picking hex codes.
  • The filters know what they contain. Colour and size are separate facets with sensible values in each.
Why this matters Every personalization decision downstream is made against this catalogue. If colour and size are muddled into one field, nothing can recommend by colour, filter by size, or learn that someone prefers navy. Most failed personalization projects fail here — quietly, months before anyone notices the recommendations are poor.

Act two · about three minutes

Show some intent

You are still anonymous. But you are no longer random — you have looked at things, and what you looked at says something.

Do this. Open three or four products from the same part of the catalogue. Linger on one. Add something to the basket and leave it there.

Then open Just for you in the nav.

  • Recommendation rows say what produced them. Above each one: the algorithm and the campaign id. You can open that campaign in your panel and check it is telling the truth.
  • The basket survives. Close the tab, come back, still there — and so is the intent behind it.
  • Some rows are empty, and say so. Those are the ones that need history rather than a catalogue.
Why this matters This is the state most of your traffic is actually in: interested, unidentified, about to leave. What a platform can do here — before a login, before an email address — is the difference between personalization and a loyalty programme.

Act three · about four minutes

Become someone

The moment an anonymous session turns into a person, everything from the first two acts should attach to them. Worth watching closely — it is where most integrations lose the thread.

Do this. Use the services link in the header and book something. Then check out; the basket you left in act two is still waiting.

Now open User Profiles in your Insider One panel and find yourself.

  • The choice you made is on your profile as a durable attribute, not only as an event that happened once.
  • Your browsing became an attribute too. Preferred category was never typed by anyone — it was derived from act two.
  • Revenue and line items land a minute or two after checkout.
  • The session you began as a stranger is now attached to you. That join is the whole game.
Why this matters A profile that only knows what someone typed into a form is a mailing list. A profile that knows what they did before they said who they were is a customer record — and it is what every later message, offer and recommendation is built on.

Then try breaking it

The line under the search box is a dare. Take it. Search for something this shop plainly does not sell and see what comes back instead of a dead end.

How to see what is happening

The pill in the bottom right corner opens an on-page console showing every payload as it leaves the browser — page type, attributes, basket, events. It is not part of the product; it is here so you can check our work rather than take it on trust. ?debug=0 hides it.

If you plan to build campaigns against these pages, install the Insider One Chrome extension — the visual editor needs it.

What you will not see yet, and why

Affinities and predictive segments read as Not Available on a new profile. They are built from around thirty days of behaviour, so a profile created ten minutes ago has nothing to calculate from. Worth knowing rather than working around: it is also what the first month of a real deployment looks like.

Anything computed by this website rather than by the platform is labelled as such wherever it appears. If a block does not say where it came from, it came from the product catalogue.

Stuck, or curious

There is an Ask a question link at the top of every page. Use it — questions from people exploring on their own are the ones we most want, because there is nobody sitting beside you to ask.

And tell us what you think when you are done.
