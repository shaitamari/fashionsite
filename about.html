Nine things to try

Before they tell you anything

Most of your traffic is anonymous and most of it leaves that way. What a platform can do here — before a login, before an email address — is the difference between personalization and a loyalty programme.

One

Arrive the way a customer would

Open the ad, then click through it.

Compare the homepage you land on with the one you get by typing the address.

Why it matters, and how it is done

Why. Paid traffic is expensive and it usually lands on a page built for everyone. Someone who clicked an ad for coats has told you something, and a homepage that ignores it wastes the click.

How. One campaign, one condition — arrived from paid social. It reads which storefront it is on and dresses the page accordingly, so twelve brands share a single campaign rather than twelve. The copy and the image live in the platform, not in the site.

Two

Search for a colour, not a product

Then open a collection and use the filters.

Or start here:

Count the results. There are fewer than you expect.

How we did it

Eureka search and category listing, running on a 17,000-record product feed. Colour and size are separate fields in the catalogue, which is why the swatches and the facets know what they contain.

Three

Browse a few things, then abandon a basket

Open Just for you afterwards.

Or jump straight to a page with good ones: this product

Look above each row of products before you look at the products.

How we did it

Smart Recommender campaigns, one per surface. Each row names its algorithm and its campaign id, so you can open that campaign in your panel and check it says what we say it says.

Some rows are empty on purpose. Those are the ones that need history rather than a catalogue.

Four

Put the same thing in your basket twice

In two different sizes. Then go to the basket.

The shop says something about it.

Why it matters, and how it is done

Why. Two sizes of one item is a return already in progress. The shop can help now — or find out in a fortnight when one comes back. Offering free returns before checkout is service, and it costs less than the return.

How. A rule reads the basket and looks for one style in two sizes. A Web Template fires on it. The message is chosen by the platform, not written into the page.

Five

Look at several things and add none of them

Three or four products, take your time, leave the basket empty.

Something turns up.

Why it matters, and how it is done

Why. This is most of your traffic — interested, unidentified, about to leave. Every visit that ends here is one you never get to follow up. Trading something small for an email address is the difference between a session and a customer.

How. Product views, time on site and an empty basket — the same signals the platform’s own purchase-intent model reads. A rule thresholds them and a template does the asking.

The moment they become someone

One thing given, and it should follow them everywhere — every page, every channel, every later decision. This is where most integrations lose the thread.

Six

Book something, then buy something

Use the services link in the header. Then check out — the basket from act three is still there.

Now open User Profiles in your panel and find yourself.

One of the attributes on your profile was never typed by anyone.

How we did it

The booking writes a durable attribute rather than only an event. Preferred category is derived from what you browsed in act three. Revenue and line items land a minute or so after checkout.

The session you started as a stranger is attached to the person you became. That join is the part worth checking.

Seven

Sign in and give yourself a tier

Open the account page, set your membership to Gold, save. Then look at the top of any page.

It follows you around.

Why it matters, and how it is done

Why. Tier is only useful if everything downstream can see it — the site, the email, the message, the recommendation. Held on the profile rather than in one campaign, it is available everywhere at once, and the VIP flag is derived from it rather than typed by anyone.

How. The profile holds which tier you are in. A lookup table holds what each tier is worth — the discount, the free-shipping threshold, the points multiplier. One is about you, the other is about the programme. Change the table and every campaign that quotes it updates, rather than someone re-editing twelve of them.

After they have gone

The session ends; the relationship does not. What happens next is built from what they did, sent on the channel they respond to, and timed to them rather than to a schedule.

Eight · the one worth the login

Then go and read the email you would have been sent

1. Open three or four products here. Do not buy anything.

2. Open the console — the pill in the bottom right — and click Copy profile ID.

3. Open the email in the browse abandonment journey.

4. Top right of the preview it says Previewing as Anonymous. Change the user, choose Profile ID, paste yours in, and reload.

The product in that email is the one you were just looking at. Its picture, its name, its link.

Why it matters, and how it is done

Nothing in that email was written for you. The product name, the image and the link are tokens, resolved per recipient at the moment of sending. Paste a different profile id in and the whole thing changes. There is one email, and it is different for everyone who receives it.

And the site did not calculate any of it. Those values are derived by the platform from ordinary page views — last visited product, its image, its URL, its category. The shop reported that a page was viewed. Working out which one mattered, and keeping it, happened here.

Now go back to the canvas and look at the whole thing. There is an entry condition, a wait, and a check that removes anyone who has bought in the meantime — because the worst abandonment email is the one that arrives after the purchase.

The split is the part worth pausing on. One arm routes with Next Best Channel: a model choosing the channel this person has responded to before. The other routes with the Decision Agent, which decides per send — which channel, at what time, with which variant — inside the goal and the guardrails set for it. Sometimes it decides not to send at all, and the restraint is the point: intelligence is not always about action.

Both arms are running on the same journey and the same data, so you can see which one is winning rather than take anyone’s word for it.

And the journey itself was not built by hand. It was described in a sentence and assembled by the Journey Agent — starter, waits, checks, channels, wired and connected. That saves an afternoon, which is the obvious part. The less obvious part is that it brings the patterns with it: the exit condition, the wait before the send, the check that stops the email chasing someone who has already bought. Those are the things people forget, and forgetting them is what makes an abandonment programme annoy customers instead of recovering them.

Nine · for the ones that need time

And if you want to see the journeys that run on time

Some journeys do not run on clicks. They run on dates — reorder before someone runs out, come back a year after they bought the thing they buy once a year. Neither is visible in a ten-minute session, so write yourself some history and they have something to fire on.

Then look at your profile again. It has a memory now.

Why it matters, and how it is done

Why. The most valuable journeys run on time rather than on clicks: reorder before someone runs out, come back a year after they bought the thing they buy once a year. Neither is visible in a ten-minute session, and neither can be faked convincingly at the point of use — so the history has to be real.

How. Purchases are stored as an array on the profile, one entry per item with its own date. A journey anchors on a date inside that array and the message is built from it. Buy the same thing twice and the gap between becomes the interval we expect next time — which beats any table of shelf lives, because a household of five gets through shampoo faster than one person does.

Everything that page writes goes through the same code a real checkout uses, so a journey cannot tell the difference.

Two more things

The line under the search box is a dare — take it. And the pill in the bottom right opens a console showing every payload as it leaves the browser, so you can check our work rather than take it on trust.

Install the Insider One Chrome extension — the visual campaign editor needs it.

Before you wonder

Affinities and predictive segments read as Not Available on a profile made ten minutes ago. They need about thirty days of behaviour — which is also what month one of a real deployment looks like.

The panel is read-only here. When you want to build something, that is a conversation rather than a limitation.
