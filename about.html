Three things to try

One · two minutes

Search for a colour, not a product

Then open a collection and use the filters.

Or start here:

Count the results. There are fewer than you expect.

How we did it

Eureka search and category listing, running on a 17,000-record product feed. Colour and size are separate fields in the catalogue, which is why the swatches and the facets know what they contain.

Two · three minutes

Browse a few things, then abandon a basket

Open Just for you in the nav afterwards.

Or jump straight to a page with good ones: this product

Look above each row of products before you look at the products.

How we did it

Smart Recommender campaigns, one per surface. Each row names its algorithm and its campaign id, so you can open that campaign in your panel and check it says what we say it says.

Some rows are empty on purpose. Those are the ones that need history rather than a catalogue.

Three · four minutes

Book something, then buy something

Use the services link in the header. Then check out — the basket from act two is still there.

Now open User Profiles in your panel and find yourself.

One of the attributes on your profile was never typed by anyone.

How we did it

The booking writes a durable attribute rather than only an event. Preferred category is derived from what you browsed in act two. Revenue and line items land a minute or so after checkout.

The session you started as a stranger is attached to the person you became. That join is the part worth checking.

Two more things

The line under the search box is a dare — take it. And the pill in the bottom right opens a console showing every payload as it leaves the browser, so you can check our work rather than take it on trust.

Before you wonder

Affinities and predictive segments read as Not Available on a profile made ten minutes ago. They need about thirty days of behaviour — which is also what month one of a real deployment looks like.

The panel is read-only here. When you want to build something, that is a conversation rather than a limitation.

Stuck, or curious

Ask a question is at the top of every page. Use it — there is nobody sitting beside you to ask, which is exactly why we want the question.
